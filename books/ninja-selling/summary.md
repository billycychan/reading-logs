# Evolving Summary

## Principle 1: Personal Mastery

The first principle argues that successful selling begins with the internal
condition of the salesperson. The Ninja system depends on three elements:
mind-set, skill set, and action. Skills and activity remain necessary, but the
wrong mind-set can undermine both by projecting fear, scarcity, anxiety, or
self-interest to customers.

Kendall distinguishes players from victims. Players accept responsibility for
their thoughts, actions, and responses even when they cannot control events.
Victims become trapped in circumstances and negative feedback loops. Emotional
energy is divided into high-positive, low-positive, high-negative, and
low-negative quadrants. Effective performance occurs in a focused,
high-positive state described as a nonanxious presence, while low-positive
energy supports recovery.

Daily routines and pre-performance rituals help regulate this energy. The
Ninja morning routine combines gratitude, positive reading, affirmations or
visualization, and two personal notes. These practices direct attention,
reinforce constructive beliefs, and strengthen relationships before external
distractions take control of the day.

The Reticular Activating System is presented as an attentional filter: people
notice what they repeatedly value, want, or fear. Because attention expands
what becomes visible, goals should describe the desired destination rather
than the unwanted condition. Repeated thoughts form beliefs; beliefs influence
actions; and actions produce results. Proper affirmations are personal,
positive, measurable, present-tense, sensory, repeated consistently, and
supported by an anchor.

The section culminates in the Net Forward Energy Ratio, which compares
productive energy directed toward a goal with doubt, distraction, and divided
attention pulling away from it. Small gains in alignment can produce much
larger gains in forward movement. Principle 1 therefore prepares the reader
for the customer-facing system by aligning emotional energy, attention,
beliefs, habits, and action.
